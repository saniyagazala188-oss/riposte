Crayon and Klue come up in almost every competitive intelligence buying conversation. They are the two best known platforms in the category, and from the outside they look alike: both track competitors, both make battlecards, both sell by demo.

They are built around different jobs, though. I spent time on both websites this week to pin down where each one leans. Here's what I found, where each is the better pick, and what a small team should look at instead.

:::note How I checked
Everything about Crayon and Klue below comes from their own websites, checked on 23 October 2026. No logos, no screenshots, no prices, because neither publishes them. If something changes, I'll update this post.
:::

## Crayon vs Klue at a glance

| | Crayon | Klue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Leans toward** | Getting intel to sales | Intel plus win-loss |
| **AI** | Scores each change by importance | Compete Agent gathers intel |
| **Battlecards** | In Salesforce, Slack, Highspot | In Salesforce deals |
| **Win-loss** | Reports by competitor | Interviews, by people or AI |
| **Impact** | Influenced revenue | Win rate, deal size |
| **Connects to** | CRM, Slack, Teams, Gong | Salesforce, Glean |
| **Pricing** | By demo | By demo |
| **Free plan** | No | No |

*Checked on crayon.co and klue.com on 23 October 2026.*

## What Crayon is best at

Crayon is built to get competitor intel to sales and prove it helped. Its homepage groups the product into four parts:

- **Analyze:** competitor monitoring, intel in your inbox, and Crayon AI, which summarizes news and ranks each change by importance.
- **Enable:** battlecards, announcements and newsletters, so the whole company hears about a competitor move.
- **Compete:** intel inside the sales tools reps already use, a leaderboard of top competitive winners, and manager coaching.
- **Measure:** win/loss by competitor, how reps use battlecards, and influenced revenue.

Its integrations page is long. It covers CRMs like Salesforce and HubSpot, chat tools like Slack and Microsoft Teams, enablement tools like Highspot, Seismic and Guru, and call recording tools like Gong and Chorus.

**Crayon is the better pick if** you have a CI or product marketing team supporting a large sales team, you want battlecards wherever reps work, and leadership wants to see revenue tied to competitive work.

## What Klue is best at

Klue puts win-loss on the same level as competitive intel. Its homepage splits the product into two halves:

- **Compete Agent:** an AI research analyst and deal assistant that collects intel and brings it to sellers, through Auto Insights and Deal Support.
- **Win-Loss Suite:** Win-Loss Capture, human expert interviews, an AI Interviewer that holds short voice conversations with buyers and sellers, Blindspot Interviews with buyers from deals you weren't part of, and a Win & Loss Story Agent.

Inside Salesforce, Klue adds a Battlecard Button on opportunities, pushes deal notes back into Klue through Win-Loss Cards, and measures how Klue use lines up with win rate, sales cycle length and deal size. It also connects to Glean.

**Klue is the better pick if** you want to know why you win and lose deals, not only what competitors are doing, and your sellers live in Salesforce.

:::tip The one question that decides it
Ask yourself: "Is my biggest gap knowing what competitors changed, or knowing why we lose to them?" The first points to Crayon. The second points to Klue.
:::

## Where they are the same

For most teams, the overlap matters more than the differences:

- **Both sell by demo.** No public pricing, no free plan and no self-serve trial on either website as of 23 October 2026. Only a quote tells you your number, and it depends on team size and add-ons.
- **Both are built for sellers.** A lot of the value sits in battlecards inside the CRM, deal support and revenue reporting.
- **Both expect someone to run them.** You get the most out of either with a CI owner or a product marketer who curates the intel every week.

## When neither is the right fit

I was a PMM tracking competitors by hand every Monday, and I can tell you the honest answer for a lot of teams: you don't need an enterprise platform yet.

Crayon and Klue are probably more than you need if:

- You are one to five marketers, or a founder doing the marketing.
- You track a handful of competitors, not twenty.
- Your sales team is small, or there isn't one yet, so CRM battlecards and rep leaderboards won't get used.
- You want to try a tool on your real competitors today, without a sales call.

In that case, look at the lighter tools in [our guide to competitive intelligence tools](/blog/competitive-intelligence-tools). Kompyte, offered alongside Semrush, sits in the middle, with battlecards and automated workflows. Visualping has a free plan for up to 5 pages and summarizes each change with AI, though you still decide what it means for your product and write the response. If you're the only marketer, I've written a separate guide to [competitive intelligence tools for a solo product marketer](/blog/competitive-intelligence-tools-solo-product-marketers).

And this is where Riposte, the tool I build, fits. It checks competitors' pricing pages, release notes, blogs and sitemaps every morning, explains each real change for your product, and drafts the response: what to create, who owns it and where to share it. It's free during the beta. It doesn't do CRM integrations or win-loss interviews, so if you need those, Crayon or Klue is the better choice.

If you're comparing one of them in depth, I keep a page on [Crayon alternatives](/alternatives/crayon) and one on [Klue alternatives](/alternatives/klue).

## Start with a battlecard, whichever tool you pick

Whatever you choose, the output sales actually uses is the battlecard. If you don't have one yet, you can draft one in a couple of minutes with the [free battlecard generator](/tools/battlecard-generator). Enter your product and a competitor's website, and it drafts a one-page card you can then edit.

Then, when you buy a platform, you already know what a good card looks like for your deals, which makes any demo easier to judge.

## Wrapping up

Crayon and Klue are both serious platforms, and the choice between them comes down to one thing: Crayon is stronger on getting intel to sales and measuring it, and Klue is stronger on win-loss. If you're a large sales organization, book demos with both and test them on your own top three competitors.

If you're a small team, start lighter. The goal is the same either way: know what changed, decide what it means, and respond before your next deal does.
