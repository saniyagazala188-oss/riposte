New blog posts are easy to notice. Rewritten pages are not. One of the biggest content moves a competitor can make is changing what an old page is *for*, and it usually happens without anyone noticing.

## What a search intent change looks like

Imagine a competitor has a guide called "A 10-point checklist for your first competitive intel program". It ranks well, and people read it to learn.

One week, the same address has a new title: "The 10 best competitive intelligence tools in 2026, compared". The headings are now product names and a pricing table. Same page, same links pointing to it, but a new job. It used to teach, and now it sells.

That is a shift from **informational** intent (people learning) to **commercial** intent (people choosing a tool). The competitor is now in front of buyers who are ready to decide, using authority the page already had.

## Why most teams miss it

When I tracked competitors by hand, I compared lists of their pages week to week. A rewritten page has the same address, so it looked like nothing had changed. At best, we noticed it had "come back" and assumed it was "optimized". Nobody opened it to see how.

## How to catch it

1. **Watch the sitemap dates.** Most sites publish a sitemap at `/sitemap.xml`, with a "last updated" date for each page. A date that moves on an old blog post or guide is your first clue.
2. **Save the page outline.** For each important content page, keep its title, meta description and headings. That is enough to see the angle without storing the whole page.
3. **Compare before and after.** When the date moves, read the outline again. Did the title gain words like "best", "vs", "alternatives" or "pricing"? Did the headings turn into product names? That is the shift.
4. **Ignore mass updates.** Some sites stamp every page with a new date whenever they redeploy. If hundreds of dates move at once, it's not a rewrite.

## What to publish in response

- **If you have no page for that buying question,** write one. Make it an honest comparison and lead with what makes you different.
- **If you have one,** update it and make sure it covers the same options the competitor now lists.
- **Brief sales,** because buyers who read that page will show up with its framing.
