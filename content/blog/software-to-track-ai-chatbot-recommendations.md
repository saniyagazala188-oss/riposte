Some buyers now ask an AI assistant "what's the best tool for…" before they visit any vendor's website. For a product marketer, that raises a new question: when they ask, does the answer name us, or a competitor?

Software that tracks AI chatbot recommendations answers that question. This guide covers how these tools work, what to look for, and six options: five standalone AI visibility tools and one competitive intelligence tool. I checked each one on its own website on 27 October 2026. I build one of them, Riposte, and I'll be clear about where the others are stronger.

## How AI chatbot tracking software works for B2B marketers

Every tool in this guide does some version of three things:

1. **Asks the questions buyers ask.** You give it prompts like "best competitor tracking tool for a small marketing team", and it asks them in one or more AI assistants, usually on a schedule.
2. **Records what the answer says.** Which brands are named, in what order, and whether your product is actually recommended or just mentioned.
3. **Shows the sources.** When an assistant searches the web, the answer relies on pages: review sites, comparison pages, lists, vendor pages. Knowing them tells you where to show up.

It helps to keep three outcomes apart:

- **A mention:** the answer names your brand.
- **A recommendation:** the answer suggests your product as a good choice for the question.
- **A cited source:** one of your pages is used as a source, even if your brand isn't recommended.

A tool that only counts mentions can make you look better than you are. The recommendation is what moves a buyer.

## Core features to look for in an AI recommendation tracker

- **Which AI engines it covers.** Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Microsoft Copilot, Claude. Your buyers may use more than one.
- **Prompt-level results.** Not just a score, but each answer, so you can see why an assistant picked a competitor.
- **Sources behind each answer.** The sites to get listed on, and the pages to improve.
- **Competitor comparison.** Your share of answers next to each competitor's.
- **A trend over time.** AI answers change from run to run, so one result means little.
- **A path to action.** Does it help you respond, for example with a content brief, or stop at the report?

### Peec AI

Peec AI is AI search analytics for marketing teams and agencies. Its plans track prompts daily across three models you choose from ChatGPT, Google AI Mode, AI Overviews, Microsoft Copilot, Gemini and Naver AI, with more models as add-ons and up to 13 on Enterprise. All plans include unlimited users.

**Good fit if** several people need access and you want daily tracking across a few engines.

### Otterly.ai

Otterly.ai monitors ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot on every plan, with Gemini, Google AI Mode and Claude as add-ons. Plans start at $29 a month for 15 prompts.

**Good fit if** you want to start small and cheap, with Perplexity included.

### ZipTie.ai

ZipTie.ai covers seven engines, including Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity and Bing's AI answers. It separates mentions (your brand named in the answer) from citations (your page used as a source), tracks sentiment on mentions, and keeps the sources behind each answer, including Reddit threads and YouTube videos. Pricing is usage-based, at about a cent per engine check.

**Good fit if** you care most about sources and citations across many engines.

### Profound

Profound covers eight engines, including ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Gemini, and says it draws insights from the consumer experience, not API outputs. It adds prompt volumes, which show what people actually ask AI platforms, citation analysis, and agents that research and write content. It says it's built for enterprise teams.

**Good fit if** you're an enterprise team that wants AI visibility, demand data and content workflows in one place.

### Semrush AI Visibility Toolkit

Semrush's AI Visibility Toolkit covers ChatGPT and Google AI Mode, with brand performance (share of voice and sentiment), prompt research, prompt tracking and competitor research. It costs $99 a month and has no free trial.

**Good fit if** your team already lives in Semrush and wants AI visibility next to its SEO data.

## Turning AI visibility into action: the competitive intelligence approach

A visibility report tells you that a competitor is recommended and you aren't. For a product marketer, that's the start of the work. You still have to work out why, and decide what to change.

That's the gap Riposte tries to close. It's a competitive intelligence tool, so AI visibility sits next to daily tracking of your competitors' websites, blogs and pricing pages:

- **It asks your buyers' questions in Gemini with Google Search**, every week, and records the products named, the sites the answers relied on and your share next to competitors.
- **For each gap, "Why are they winning?"** shows what the answer says the winner is known for and which sites it leaned on, and, for competitors you track, what they changed on their own site recently.

## How to choose the right tool for your team

**SEO and content teams that need many AI engines and detailed attribution** should start with a standalone tool. Profound and ZipTie.ai cover the most engines on their standard offers, Peec AI's Enterprise plan goes up to 13 models and every plan includes unlimited users, Otterly.ai is the cheapest way to start, and Semrush fits if you already use it.

**Small product marketing teams of 1 to 5 people** who also track competitors may get more from a tool that connects AI visibility to competitor changes and drafts the next step. That's where Riposte fits, if Gemini coverage is enough for now.

**The trade-off** is breadth versus depth. A standalone tool tells you how you show up across many AI assistants. A competitive intelligence tool covers fewer engines but ties each gap to what competitors changed and what to write next. Some teams use both: a standalone tool for coverage, and a CI tool for the response.

Whichever you choose, compare tools on your own buyer questions. Every vendor's sample report looks good. Your own prompts show what you'd actually learn. To compare competitor tracking tools more broadly, see the [compare hub](/alternatives).
