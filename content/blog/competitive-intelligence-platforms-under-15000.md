"Is it under budget?" is usually the first question about a competitive intelligence tool, and often the hardest to answer. Many CI vendors don't publish prices, and the ones that do price by very different things: seats, competitors, pages or products.

So for this guide I checked each vendor's own pricing page on 27 October 2026 and wrote down two things: the price, if it's public, and what the plan actually allows. Where a vendor doesn't publish a price, I say so instead of guessing whether it's under $15,000. I build one of these tools, Riposte, and I'll be clear about its limits too.

## How competitive intelligence tools are priced

Before comparing numbers, check what the number is based on. The tools here use four different models:

- **By competitors and licenses.** Kompyte's plans track 10, 20 or unlimited companies, with 25, 100 or unlimited user licenses. Its plans page says price depends on the competitors tracked, the licenses, and whether you need single sign-on.
- **By pages and checks.** Visualping charges for how many pages you watch and how often it checks them, from 5 pages on the free plan to 500 on its largest Business plan.
- **By products and sales channels.** Prisync charges for how many products you track, and extra for each sales channel. It's built for online stores.
- **By organization, with unlimited users.** Contify offers unlimited user access, so you aren't paying per seat, but it doesn't publish a price.

The model matters as much as the price. A tool that charges per seat gets expensive the moment you want sales to read the intel. A tool that limits competitors gets expensive when your market is crowded.

:::tip Ask for the number before the demo
If a vendor doesn't publish prices, ask for a ballpark by email before you book a demo. A 30-minute call is a lot to spend on a tool that turns out to be five times your budget.
:::

## The tools, one by one

### Visualping

Visualping watches any page you choose and alerts you when it changes. It writes an AI summary of each change, and you can set criteria so it only alerts you about changes that matter to you.

- **Free plan:** 5 pages, checked every 60 minutes.
- **Personal plans:** $120 to $600 a year, for 10 to 40 pages.
- **Business plans:** $1,200 to $3,000 a year, for 200 to 500 pages, checked as often as every 2 minutes.

**Good fit if** you want to watch a few competitor pricing pages cheaply. **Keep in mind** that you add every page yourself, and you still decide what each change means for your product.

### Prisync

Prisync tracks competitors' product prices for online stores. Its pricing page says it's built for ecommerce businesses.

- **Plans:** from $99 a month (100 products) to $799 a month (5,000 products), depending on the model.
- **Competitors:** the Professional plans list unlimited competitors.
- **Extras:** additional sales channels cost $100 to $200 a month each, and API access adds 20%.
- **Trial:** 14 days, no credit card.

**Good fit if** you sell products online and compete on price. **Not a fit if** you're a B2B SaaS company tracking competitors' pricing pages, launches and messaging: it's built around product catalogs.

### Riposte

Riposte is the tool I build, for teams of 1 to 5 marketers. Every morning it checks your competitors' pricing pages, release notes, blogs and sitemaps, ignores noise, and turns each real change into a signal: what changed, why it matters for your product, and what to do.

- **Price:** free during the beta, with no final price yet.
- **What the beta allows:** up to 5 competitors.
- **Alerts:** high-impact changes by email and Slack, plus a weekly digest.
- **Integrations:** Slack and email only. There's no CRM integration.

**Good fit if** you're a small team that wants the response drafted, not just the alert. **Not a fit if** you need more than 5 competitors today, or battlecards inside your CRM.

### Kompyte

Kompyte is competitive intelligence software offered with Semrush, for go-to-market teams. It tracks competitors' websites, builds battlecards and reports, and has automated workflows for sharing intel.

- **Plans:** Essentials (10 companies, 25 user licenses), Professional (20 companies, 100 licenses) and Unlimited (unlimited companies and licenses, with SSO).
- **Price:** not published. It offers annual plans, and says shorter terms "may be available for a premium".

**Good fit if** you already use Semrush or want battlecards for a sales team. Ask for a quote to see if it fits your budget. I keep a page on [Kompyte alternatives](/alternatives/kompyte) if you're comparing it with lighter options.

### Contify

Contify describes itself as an AI-native market and competitive intelligence platform for organization-wide teams, tracking markets, industries, competitors and key accounts.

- **Price:** not published.
- **Users:** unlimited user access, so no per-seat cost.
- **Trial:** a 7-day free trial, or a demo.

**Good fit if** several teams across the company need market and competitor news. Ask for a quote to see if it fits under $15,000.

### Klue and Crayon

Klue and Crayon are the best-known enterprise platforms, built for revenue teams, with battlecards inside sales tools and win-loss features. Neither publishes prices, and both start with a demo, so I can't say whether either fits under $15,000. If you're weighing the two, I've compared them in [Crayon vs Klue](/blog/crayon-vs-klue).

## Alerts or action kits: what are you paying for?

Most tools in this price range send alerts. That's useful, but an alert is the start of the work, not the end. Someone still has to:

1. **Decide if it matters.** Is a new pricing tier a threat, or noise?
2. **Decide what to do.** Update the battlecard, brief sales, change a comparison page?
3. **Write it.** Start the draft, and get it to the right people.

When you compare tools, ask which of those steps each one does for you. Some sort changes by impact (high, medium, low) so you read the important ones first. Some go further and draft the response. In Riposte, one click turns a change into an action kit: what to create, who owns it, where to share it, and a first draft to edit.

If you're the only marketer, this matters more than the feature list. I've written about it in [competitive intelligence tools for a solo product marketer](/blog/competitive-intelligence-tools-solo-product-marketers).

## Don't forget AI visibility

Some buyers now ask an AI assistant which tool to use before they visit any website. Tracking competitors' pages won't show you what those answers say.

What to look for:

- **Your buying questions**, written without brand names, asked in an AI assistant.
- **Which brands get named**, and how often you appear next to competitors.
- **Which sites the answers rely on**, because those are worth getting listed on.

Riposte asks your prompts in Gemini with Google Search every week and records the brands named and the sites cited. AI answers change from run to run, so treat it as a trend.

## How to choose under a $15,000 budget

1. **Start with your team.** A dedicated CI manager can run a full platform. A busy PMM needs something that does the checking alone.
2. **Count your competitors.** Then check what each plan allows: competitors, pages or products.
3. **Check the sources.** Pricing pages, release notes, blogs and feeds matter most for B2B SaaS.
4. **Check how intel reaches people.** Email, Slack, a CRM? Pick the tool that fits where your team already works.
5. **Get the real price.** For quote-only tools, ask for a number before the demo.
6. **Test it on your own competitors.** Use a free plan, a trial or a beta for a week before any annual contract.

## Wrapping up

Under $15,000 a year, the first job is separating what you can verify from what you can't. Visualping, Prisync and Riposte publish what they cost and what they allow. Kompyte, Contify, Klue and Crayon need a quote. Whichever you test, judge it on your own competitors, and on how much of the work after the alert it takes off your plate.
